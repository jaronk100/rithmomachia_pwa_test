THE IDEA

Two armies of numbered pieces. You don't capture by landing on a piece — you capture by proving an arithmetical relationship to it. You win by getting three pieces into enemy territory whose numbers form a progression.

MOVEMENT

Straight lines only. Never diagonally, never jumping, and the distance is exact.

  • Circle — exactly 1 square
  • Triangle — exactly 2 squares
  • Square — exactly 3 squares
  • Pyramid (gold rim) — 1, 2 or 3; it may move as any shape it still contains

A TURN

Move one piece. Then every capture your position now makes happens automatically. Your piece never moves onto the captured square — the enemy piece simply leaves the board, for good.

THE FOUR CAPTURES

Encounter — you could reach an enemy of the same value.
your 25 can reach their 25

Ambush — two of your pieces could reach the same enemy, and their values add or subtract to it.
your 6 and 6 both reach their 12

Assault — you and an enemy share a row or column with only empty squares between. Count them (n). Capture if your value × n, or your value ÷ n, equals theirs.
your 8, four empty squares from their 32

Siege — an enemy piece has no legal move left and at least one blocker is yours. Board edges count as walls.

THE PYRAMIDS

A pyramid is a stack of separate pieces. The light one is squares 36 and 25, triangles 16 and 9, circles 4 and 1 — total 91. The dark one is squares 64 and 49, triangles 36 and 25, circle 16 — total 190. Each component grants a move distance, so a pyramid that loses its circles can no longer step one square.

Tap any pyramid to see inside it — your own needs a second tap, since the first one selects it.

WINNING

Get three pieces into the enemy half, in a straight line, evenly spaced, values rising or falling in a progression:

  • Arithmetic — equal gaps  2, 4, 6
  • Geometric — equal ratios  2, 4, 8
  • Harmonic — a : c = (b−a) : (c−b)  9, 15, 45

Capturing enough pieces also wins, depending on the setting. And if you ever have no legal move, you lose.

LIGHT AND DARK

The sources call these armies Evens and Odds, after the numbers each is built from — the first four evens (2, 4, 6, 8) or odds (3, 5, 7, 9). Only the circles keep that parity; the triangles and squares scramble it. Dark's pieces are worth more in total, which is less of an advantage than it sounds: it means your rival scores faster off them. Dark moves first to compensate for Light's more flexible small numbers.
